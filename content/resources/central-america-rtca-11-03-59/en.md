## No single approval, one shared rulebook

Central American integration has harmonized the **requirements** for registering a human medicine, not the **approval**. RTCA 11.03.59:18 states its purpose as setting the conditions and requirements under which the sanitary registration of human medicines will be granted, and it applies to medicines that people or companies make or import to sell in the territory of the member states.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

The same regulation says the administrative procedure for registration, renewal and changes follows the internal legislation of each member state.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> In practice that means six separate files, six fees and six registration numbers. The work you can share is the content of the dossier.

The RTCA was approved by the Council of Ministers of Economic Integration (COMIECO) through Resolution 446-2021, signed on 28 April 2021 by the ministers of Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama. It replaced the earlier version, RTCA 11.03.59:11, and the earlier mutual recognition procedure, both from Resolution 333-2013, and it entered into force on **3 January 2022**.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## What the RTCA harmonizes

- **The dossier.** Section 7 lists the requirements: application, powers of attorney, WHO-type Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (or Certificate of Free Sale plus GMP certificates), manufacturing contract when a third party manufactures, full formula, monograph, validated analytical methods, finished product specifications, labels, stability study, safety and efficacy studies where needed, and proof of payment.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Document rules.** Certificates must be valid when filed, and if no validity is stated they count as valid for two years from issue. Documents must be originals or legalized copies, in Spanish or translated.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Validity and renewal.** A registration lasts five years and can be renewed for equal periods. Renewal can be requested any time before expiry without losing the number.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **One registration per site.** If the same product is made in different affiliates or countries, a registration is needed per country or manufacturing laboratory. El Salvador is the exception because it uses the figure of alternate manufacturing.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Refusal and cancellation grounds**, exceptions to registration (such as donations and declared emergencies), and the classification of post-registration changes in Annex 1, split between changes that need prior approval and changes that only need a notification.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

The RTCA replaces each country's own registration, renewal and change requirements for human medicines, except for the national rules on intellectual property and bioequivalence.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## What the RTCA does not harmonize

- **Procedure, fees and timelines.** Left to national law, as is proof of payment.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Legalization of foreign documents.** Each document issued abroad must be legalized under the specific national rules.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Intellectual property and therapeutic equivalence.** Each country applies its own rules.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Some product types.** Magistral preparations are excluded. For nutritional supplements, homeopathic products, radiopharmaceuticals, biological and biotechnological medicines and orphan drugs, each country applies its own law until a regional regulation exists. A country with no national law for biologics applies the RTCA to them.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Sanctions.** Each country applies its own penalty rules.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

The text also carries country notes. Costa Rica, Guatemala, El Salvador and Honduras accept a Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product from a stringent regulatory authority that approved the product, even if it is not the country of origin, for the first registration of a product whose safety and efficacy is not yet documented in the official literature. A finished product unit is required only in Nicaragua and Panama. In Panama, long term stability studies must cover climatic zone IVb.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Analytical standards and samples are requested after registration, according to each country's law.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## Mutual recognition: what the resolution actually says

Annex II of Resolution 446-2021 sets the procedure for mutual recognition of sanitary registrations of human medicines. Its scope is one sentence: recognition applies to every sanitary registration of medicines **originating in the member states** ("medicamentos originarios de los Estados Parte").<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> The RTCA defines the country of origin as the country where the product is manufactured.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Panama's Dirección Nacional de Farmacia y Drogas reads it the same way in its official questions and answers: medicines made in one of the Central American countries must request registration in Panama through mutual recognition, meeting the RTCA requirements.<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>

So for a medicine made in Europe, India or the United States, do not plan on recognition. Plan on a national registration in each country.

When it applies, the procedure works like this:<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

1. File proof of payment and an application signed by the profesional responsable and the holder or its legal representative. In Panama, the application also needs the endorsement (refrendo) of the Colegio Nacional de Farmacéuticos.
2. Attach a legalized power of attorney to a person who resides permanently in the recognizing country, and the original legalized Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product from the country of origin, including the formula and GMP compliance.
3. Deliver a copy of the full dossier with a sworn statement that it matches the one filed in the country of registration. Most countries ask for it when they hand over the approval; Panama asks for it at the start.
4. The authority decides within **10 business days**, or **30 business days in Panama**, and assigns a number with the code RM plus the country initial. The recognition keeps the validity of the original registration.

Recognition can be refused, for example, when the sale mode approved in the country of origin differs from the one in the recognizing country, when the product does not meet GMP, or, in Panama, when stability in climatic zone IVB is not shown. Products that need bioequivalence studies, and products under data protection, stay subject to each country's rules. A product cannot be marketed without notifying the changes made to the original registration.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## The PAHO-supported joint evaluation mechanism

The joint evaluation mechanism is a different tool. It does not recognize an existing registration. It lets the participating authorities review one dossier together.

- PAHO announced on 28 July 2020 that the mechanism, launched with its support by the authorities of Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua and Panama, would receive dossiers from **1 August 2020**. In the first stage it accepted chemical synthesis medicines that are not required to show bioequivalence in any Central American country.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- The central procedure had no fee, but local fees must be paid in each country, and the applicant must present the documentation required for registration in all participating countries. The mechanism respects each country's rules and administrative procedures.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- In June 2021 PAHO said the mechanism ensures a single technical review, aiming to complete the procedure in less than three months.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>
- In August 2022 Guatemala's official news agency reported that the health ministry had joined the mechanism, and that after verification each product could enter each participating country with that country's own registration.<sup><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>
- In June 2022 PAHO described the joint review as a commission of the participating countries that checks whether a medicine meets the Central American technical regulations, ending in a single technical review that must be attached to the registration process of each participating country. That meeting of the region's regulatory directors, the last public update we found, reviewed the status of the joint evaluation of dossiers.<sup><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

We found no official notice after 2022 on whether the mechanism still receives dossiers, how many it has reviewed, or its current product scope. Treat it as an option to confirm with the authorities, not as a planned route.

## Panama's position

Panama took part in drafting the RTCA, its minister signed Resolution 446-2021, and the text includes notes that apply only to Panama.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> That is a change from 2013, when Resolution 333-2013 stated it would not enter into force for Panama until COMIECO issued a further act.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

At national level, Law 419 of 1 February 2024 replaced (subrogated) Law 1 of 2001 on medicines,<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup> and Executive Decree 27 of 10 May 2024 regulates it.<sup><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup> The decree says:<sup><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>

- To obtain a registration, a product must meet Article 29 of Law 419 and the norms adopted by the Dirección Nacional de Farmacia y Drogas (DNFD) in the RTCA or other international standards.
- Applications are filed as a digital file on the DNFD platform, presented by a lawyer, with the endorsement of the responsible pharmacist and of the Colegio Nacional de Farmacéuticos.
- An **abbreviated procedure** applies to medicines registered and marketed in countries whose regulatory authority is recognized as high standard by WHO or PAHO and listed by executive decree. Prior laboratory testing is not required in that route, but the holder or manufacturer must arrange quality control testing of the first imported lot within six months.

The DNFD also states that registering a product from abroad requires a legal representative residing in Panama, authorized by the product's holder.<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup> U.S. government trade notes describe the same law as a way to fast track products approved by authorities such as the FDA and EMA.<sup><a href="#source-10">10</a></sup>

## Practical sequencing

1. **Check scope first.** Confirm your product is not in a category left to national law (for example biologics or homeopathics), and check bioequivalence and patent or data protection rules in each target country.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
2. **Build one core dossier to section 7 of the RTCA**, then add the country notes: zone IVb stability for Panama, a product unit for Nicaragua and Panama, and the CPP options in Costa Rica, Guatemala, El Salvador and Honduras.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
3. **Decide who holds the registration in each country** and appoint the local legal representative and profesional responsable. See [what a registration holder is](/en/resources/what-is-a-registration-holder).
4. **Plan legalized originals.** Each authority needs originals or legalized copies, legalized under its own rules, and every certificate must still be valid when filed.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
5. **Pick the route per country:** a national registration, mutual recognition if the product is made in the region, Panama's abbreviated route if your product is approved by a listed high standard authority, or the joint evaluation if the authorities confirm it is open.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a>, <a href="#source-3">3</a>, <a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>
6. **Keep the files in step after approval.** Post-registration changes follow Annex 1 of the RTCA in every country where you hold a registration, and changes must be approved before renewal is granted.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
