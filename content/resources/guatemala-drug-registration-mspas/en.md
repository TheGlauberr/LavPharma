## Who regulates medicines in Guatemala

The Ministry of Public Health and Social Assistance (MSPAS) regulates the registration, manufacture, import and sale of pharmaceutical products.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Inside the Ministry, the file is handled by the **Departamento de Regulación y Control de Productos Farmacéuticos y Afines (DRCPFA)**, part of the General Directorate of Health Regulation, Surveillance and Control. The National Health Laboratory (Laboratorio Nacional de Salud, LNS) performs the quality tests.<sup><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>

In Guatemala a medicine registration is called a **registro sanitario de referencia**. Pharmaceutical specialties, biological products, radiopharmaceuticals and homeopathic products all need one.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## The rules that apply

1. **Health Code (Decree 90-97).** Its Article 167 defines the registration as the set of product specifications that serves as the reference to control the product on the market, gives it a five year term, and requires it to be done under the responsibility of a university-trained professional in the field.<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>
2. **Government Agreement 712-99**, the Regulation for the Sanitary Control of Medicines and Related Products, with its amendments. It governs the application, review, renewal and cancellation of registrations.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
3. **RTCA 11.03.59:18**, the Central American regulation on registration requirements, approved by COMIECO Resolution 446-2021 and in force since 3 January 2022. Guatemala signed it, and its Ministry of Health sat on the committee that drafted it.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>
4. **DRCPFA technical standards**, which set out specific procedures such as renewal and homologation.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a>, <a href="#source-4">4</a>, <a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

The RTCA harmonizes the technical requirements, but it leaves the procedure for registration, renewal and changes to each country's law, and keeps intellectual property and therapeutic equivalence national.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> That is why some requirements of Government Agreement 712-99, such as the patent statements, remain specific to Guatemala.

The DRCPFA has also posted on its website a **draft under review** of a new regulation that would replace Government Agreement 712-99.<sup><a href="#source-12">12</a></sup> It is a draft and is not in force; we are following it closely.

## Who signs and who holds the registration

The RTCA defines the registration holder as the natural or legal person that owns the product.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> In Guatemala, the dossier includes a document proving the legal representation that the holder grants to the natural or legal person responsible for the product, as an original or a legalized photocopy.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

The application is signed and sealed by the **chemist-pharmacist responsible for the registration**.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> That pharmacist answers for keeping the formula unchanged and for the purity of the substances in the product registered, a responsibility shared with the company's legal representative.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

The distributor matters too: the application is denied if the manufacturer or the distributor lacks the sanitary licence the regulation requires.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> See [what a registration holder is](/en/resources/what-is-a-registration-holder) to decide whose name the registration should be in.

## Step by step

1. **Set up your local team.** Grant the power of attorney to the legal representative and appoint the chemist-pharmacist responsible for the registration.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
2. **Confirm your distributor's sanitary licence**, because without it the application is denied.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
3. **Assemble the dossier** with the documents listed below.
4. **Pay the DRCPFA and LNS fees** and obtain the 63-A2 receipts that go into the dossier.<sup><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>
5. **File the application.** The DRCPFA publishes video tutorials on creating a user account and filing a registration application on its SIAMED Web platform.<sup><a href="#source-11">11</a></sup> Ministerial Agreement 263-2025 provides that paper files already in progress keep being reviewed alongside electronic ones, in order of filing.<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>
6. **Answer the review notes.** If the dossier falls short, the DRCPFA issues a deficiency note (nota de reparo). The Law for the Simplification of Administrative Requirements and Procedures allows files returned for corrections to be archived if the applicant does not move them forward for more than three months.<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>
7. **LNS testing.** In the standard route, the LNS reviews the dossier, samples and reference standards, tests the samples and issues a certificate of analysis. The DRCPFA issues the registration certificate once the analysis passes.<sup><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>
8. **Receive the registration certificate**, valid for five years.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## Documents from the foreign manufacturer

The base is section 7 of RTCA 11.03.59:18, which is the same in all six countries:<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

- Application signed and sealed by the profesional responsable. Guatemala uses form F-AS-f-04, and the formula per dosage unit goes on form F-AS-d-01.<sup><a href="#source-10">10</a></sup>
- Powers of attorney proving the legal and technical representation granted by the holder.
- WHO-type Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (CPP). If one is not issued, a Certificate of Free Sale plus a Good Manufacturing Practice certificate for each site involved in manufacturing.
- Manufacturing contract when a third party makes the product.
- Complete qualitative and quantitative formula, monograph, validated analytical methods, finished product specifications, labels or their drafts, and stability study.
- Safety and efficacy studies when these are not documented in the official literature.
- Proof of payment.

Request a CPP for Guatemala: the certificate must name the country the product will be imported into, so the same CPP cannot be used for several countries.

Guatemala adds its own requirements under Government Agreement 712-99:<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

- **Sworn statement on patents.** You must state whether any patents in force in Guatemala cover the product or its uses. If they do, you must show that you hold the patent or have the patent holder's written authorization. The application is not processed without this statement.
- **Test data protection.** For a new product, in addition to the CPP, you must provide the written consent of whoever obtained the approval in another country, or evidence that the approval took effect more than five years ago.
- **Samples and reference standards** for the LNS, with the certificate of analysis of the raw material standards. According to the DRCPFA summary table, in a standard registration of a pharmaceutical specialty the dossier is also delivered to the LNS.<sup><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>

Four general RTCA rules apply to every document: each certificate must be valid when filed (two years if it states no validity), documents are filed as originals or legalized copies, in Spanish or translated, and corrections are not accepted unless backed by the issuer.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> In its homologation standard, the DRCPFA asks for a sworn translation and for foreign documents to comply with Articles 37 and 38 of the Judiciary Law (Ley del Organismo Judicial).<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

## Shorter routes

**Homologation (Technical Standard 77).** It applies to chemically synthesized pharmaceutical specialties and vaccines already registered by a regulatory authority of high maturity and performance as defined by the WHO, according to the official lists the DRCPFA publishes.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup> Its main rules:

- The CPP or equivalent document must come from an authority on those lists, and the product type must fall within the scope the WHO recognizes for that authority.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- If the product is not sold in the country that issues the certificate, you must add a CPP or Certificate of Free Sale from a high maturity authority in a country where it is sold.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- The legal representative must notify the import of the first lot so the LNS can check its quality, and the product is subject to market sampling.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- For homologation files already pending on paper, Ministerial Agreement 263-2025 orders a simplified review of the CPP, the labelling and the stability study.<sup><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>

El Salvador has a similar recognition route on paper that is not applied today, so do not take this route's timelines for granted. Ask us how it is working in practice.

**Mutual recognition.** The procedure in Annex II of Resolution 446-2021 applies to registrations of medicines originating in the RTCA member states.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> The DRCPFA has its own form to request it (F-AS-p-01).<sup><a href="#source-10">10</a></sup> If your product is made outside Central America, this route does not apply.

**Central American joint evaluation.** In August 2022 the MSPAS joined the regional joint evaluation mechanism, in which the DRCPFA takes part in the review.<sup><a href="#source-13">13</a></sup> In our experience the mechanism only takes certain molecules, so confirm first whether yours qualifies. See the [El Salvador registration guide](/en/resources/el-salvador-drug-registration-srs) if you are also planning that country.

## Fees

Government Agreement 53-2022, in force since 1 March 2022, sets these DRCPFA fees:<sup><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>

| Service | Fee |
|---|---|
| Medicine registration | Q130.00 |
| Medicine registration renewal | Q150.00 |
| Dossier update certificate (per change) | Q50.00 |
| Mutual recognition certificate for medicines (annual) | Q750.00 |

LNS tests are charged separately, per test. For example, the same agreement sets Q400 for an organoleptic test that needs no equipment and Q2,500 for a dissolution test by high performance liquid chromatography.<sup><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup> The total depends on the tests the LNS requires for your product. Confirm the current amounts with the DRCPFA before paying.

## Validity and renewal

The registration lasts five years and is renewed at the holder's request, after the technical documentation is updated.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a>, <a href="#source-9">9</a></sup>

- **When to file.** Technical Standard 90 of 2023 allows the application to be filed up to the last day the registration is valid.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>
- **If it expires.** Government Agreement 712-99 provides that if the renewal is not filed before expiry, the DRCPFA cancels the registration.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Technical Standard 27-2010 adds that applications received after expiry are processed as new registrations.<sup><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>
- **What to file.** Technical Standard 27-2010 asks for, among other items, the application signed by the responsible pharmacist and the holder or legal representative, a sworn statement that the formula, specifications, packaging, insert, manufacturing and manufacturing site have not changed, and a current CPP or Certificate of Free Sale.<sup><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

The RTCA allows a holder to ask, within six months after expiry and with justified cause, to keep the original registration number.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> The Guatemalan rules cited above are stricter, so do not let your registration expire.
