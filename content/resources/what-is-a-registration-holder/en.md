## Key facts

| Role | What it is | Must it be local? |
|---|---|---|
| Holder (titular del registro) | The natural or legal person that owns the product<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> | The RTCA definition sets no residence requirement<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> |
| Manufacturer (laboratorio fabricante) | The authorized facility that makes the product<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> | No; it can be anywhere, and its GMP is certified by its own authority<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> |
| Legal representative (representante legal) | Person or company authorized by the holder to answer to the authority<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> | Yes, it resides in the country where the registration is filed<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> |
| Profesional responsable | Pharmacist or chemist-pharmacist responsible for the file, empowered by the holder<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> | Must be accepted by the national authority; El Salvador requires prior inscription<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> |

This article uses the Central American rules (RTCA 11.03.59:18) as the reference, with national detail only where an official source for El Salvador or Panama says so. Other Latin American countries use different terms and rules.

## Four roles, four definitions

RTCA 11.03.59:18, the registration regulation shared by Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica and Panama, defines each role:<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

- **Holder of the product or holder of the registration (titular del producto o titular del registro):** "the natural or legal person that owns the product."
- **Manufacturing laboratory (laboratorio fabricante):** an authorized entity with facilities designed to carry out all the operations involved in making pharmaceutical products.
- **Legal representative (representante legal):** a natural or legal person that resides in the country where the registration is filed, authorized by the holder through a power of attorney, to answer to the regulatory authority.
- **Profesional responsable:** a pharmacist or chemist-pharmacist responsible for the registration file before the authority, authorized by the holder or its legal representative through a power of attorney.

The RTCA also defines the **country of the holder** as the country where the holder is domiciled, separate from the country of origin, where the product is made.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> The two can differ, which is why the regulation allows a Certificate of Free Sale or a Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product to be issued by the holder's country when a product made by a third party is not sold in its country of origin.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

National rules use the same structure. El Salvador's Reglamento General de la Ley de Medicamentos defines the holder as the person in whose name the authorization is issued, and the responsible pharmacist as the one who answers for the quality and truthfulness of the product before the authority, authorized by the holder.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> Panama's Executive Decree 27 of 2024 uses the RTCA definition of profesional responsable.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

## What the holder is responsible for

The holder is the party the authority deals with for the life of the registration. Official texts tie these duties to it:

- **The product itself when a third party makes it.** Manufacturing by third parties is done under a prior contract between the holder and the manufacturer, "the holder being responsible for the product." The contract must be signed by both and commit to GMP.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a>, <a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>
- **Every change.** Post-registration changes are backed by a document issued by the holder or its legal representative, and the profesional responsable can only request them if the holder's power of attorney allows it.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> In El Salvador, a holder that changes any condition of the authorization without prior approval commits a very serious infraction.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- **Renewal statements.** At renewal, the holder, its legal representative or the empowered pharmacist swears that the product has not changed. A false sworn statement is a ground for cancellation.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Keeping a local team in place.** In El Salvador the holder must always have a profesional responsable and an apoderado responsable registered with the authority.<sup><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>
- **Quality and marketing.** In El Salvador, national manufacturers and holders of import and marketing authorizations must present a quality control certificate for each lot, and the authority can check it at the registration holder's cost. An authorization lapses if the holder does not actually market the product within five years, or if it is off the market for three consecutive years. False documents from the holder or the manufacturer are a ground for cancellation.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- **Safety reporting.** In Panama, registration holders, national and foreign laboratories and distributors must be able to report suspected adverse reactions electronically, and a local pharmacist must be designated as the contact for post-registration quality controls.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

The holder can also ask the authority to cancel its own registration.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a>, <a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

## How to change the holder

A change of holder is not a formality. Annex 1 of the RTCA lists it among the changes that need **prior approval** by the authority, and the file must include:<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

1. Proof of payment.
2. An application signed and sealed by the profesional responsable.
3. New labels for the primary and secondary packaging, or their drafts.
4. A document issued by the holder or its legal representative declaring the change.
5. A manufacturing contract with the new holder.

Panama's decree lists a similar set: an application signed by the profesional responsable, a legal document supporting the change, packaging and proof of payment.<sup><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

Compare this with a distributor. The RTCA application lists distributors separately from the holder, and adding or changing a distributor is a simple notification that needs no prior approval.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Changing the legal representative or the profesional responsable needs prior approval and a new power of attorney.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## The options for a foreign manufacturer

**1. Hold the registration yourself, as a foreign company.** The RTCA definition of holder sets no residence requirement; what must be local is the legal representative and the profesional responsable.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Panama's regulator confirms that registering from abroad requires a legal representative residing in Panama, authorized by the holder.<sup><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup> You keep ownership of the registration. You still need people in each country you can trust to file, answer observations and keep powers of attorney current.

**2. Hold it through your own local subsidiary.** You keep control inside your group and have a local entity of your own. The cost is setting up and running a company in each country, which usually only pays off where sales volume is large.

**3. Let your distributor hold it.** It is often the fastest way to start, because the distributor already has a local team. The trade off is control: in the eyes of the regulator the holder is the owner of the product.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

**4. Appoint an independent regulatory company.** An independent firm can act as legal representative and profesional responsable while you remain the holder, or hold the registration for you under a contract that obliges it to transfer the registration back. Because it does not sell the product, it has no commercial reason to keep the registration from your next distributor. You do depend on another firm and its fees, so check its track record and the transfer terms before you sign.

## Risks of letting a distributor hold your registration

- **You do not own the registration.** The regulator treats the holder as the owner of the product, and every later change needs the holder's document.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Switching distributors becomes a change of holder.** If you hold the registration, adding a new distributor is a notification. If the old distributor holds it, moving to a new one needs prior approval and a document from the current holder, which is the distributor you are leaving.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>
- **Your market access follows their decisions.** In El Salvador a registration lapses if the holder does not market the product within five years, or stops marketing it for three consecutive years.<sup><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>
- **Their compliance becomes your exposure.** False sworn statements and false documents are grounds for cancellation, whoever filed them.<sup><a href="#source-1">1</a>, <a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

If a distributor must hold the registration, write the exit into the contract: an obligation to sign the change of holder documents on request, delivery of the full dossier, and the powers of attorney you will need.
